1. Preflight Preparation & Airworthiness

Knowledge Areas:
  • Pilot privileges, limitations, currency requirements, and logbook recordkeeping[cite: 1].
  • Required aircraft certificates, inspections, ADs, and day/night VFR equipment rules (14 CFR § 91.205)[cite: 1].
  • Weight and balance calculations, performance charts, and density altitude effects[cite: 1].
Risk Management & Skills:
  • Evaluating currency vs. proficiency and managing unfamiliar avionics[cite: 1].
  • Verifying aircraft airworthiness and handling pre-flight inoperative equipment under § 91.213[cite: 1].
FAA Links: 14 CFR Part 61 14 CFR § 91.205 PHAK Ch 11 (Performance)

2. Weather Information & Aeromedical Factors

Knowledge Areas:
  • Weather briefing sources, METARs, TAFs, GFA charts, and wind/temperature aloft forecasts[cite: 1].
  • Atmospheric stability, frontal systems, icing, thunderstorms, and windshear[cite: 1].
  • Aeromedical factors: hypoxia, spatial disorientation, fatigue, and hazardous attitudes[cite: 1].
Risk Management & Skills:
  • Making sound go/no-go and continue/divert decisions based on real-time weather[cite: 1].
  • Performing personal minimums self-assessments and recognizing physiological triggers[cite: 1].
FAA Links: PHAK Ch 12 (Weather Theory) PHAK Ch 17 (Aeromedical)

3. Airport & Ground Operations

Knowledge Areas:
  • Airport diagrams, hot spots, runway incursion avoidance, and taxi/sailing rules[cite: 1].
  • Radio communication procedures, ATC phraseology, light signals, and lost comms[cite: 1].
  • Passenger brief requirements (seatbelts, sterile flight deck, emergency exit operations)[cite: 1].
Risk Management & Skills:
  • Mitigating expectation bias on taxi clearances and managing ramp distractions[cite: 1].
  • Maintaining positive directional control during ground operations without excessive braking[cite: 1].
FAA Links: AFH Ch 2 (Ground Ops) AFH Ch 7 (Airport Ops)

4. Takeoffs, Landings & Performance Maneuvers

Knowledge Areas:
  • Stabilized approach concepts, energy management, and crosswind correction techniques[cite: 1].
  • Soft-field weight transfer dynamics and short-field obstacle clearance parameters ($V_X / V_Y$)[cite: 1].
  • Aerodynamics of steep turns ($50^\circ$ bank), load factor, chandelles, and lazy eights[cite: 1].
Risk Management & Skills:
  • Managing low-altitude maneuvering risks, stall/spin awareness, and delayed go-arounds[cite: 1].
  • Executing targeted landings (within 100-200 ft) and precise commercial performance maneuvers[cite: 1].
FAA Links: AFH Ch 9 (Landings) AFH Ch 10 (Commercial Maneuvers)

5. Navigation & Airspace

Knowledge Areas:
  • Pilotage, dead reckoning, flight log preparation, and magnetic compass errors[cite: 1].
  • Ground-based (VOR) and satellite-based (GPS/RAIM) navigation system utilization[cite: 1].
  • National Airspace System (NAS) classes, Special Use Airspace (SUA), and TFRs[cite: 1].
Risk Management & Skills:
  • Mitigating automation reliance, signal loss, and spatial disorientation[cite: 1].
  • Executing prompt cross-country diversions and navigating via pre-computed headings[cite: 1].
FAA Links: PHAK Ch 15 (Airspace) PHAK Ch 16 (Navigation)

6. Slow Flight, Stalls & Emergency Operations

Knowledge Areas:
  • Relationship between angle of attack, airspeed, load factor, power settings, and yaw[cite: 1].
  • Recognition of impending/full stall cues and spin recovery procedures[cite: 1].
  • Emergency descent triggers, best glide mechanics, and system malfunction checklists[cite: 1].
Risk Management & Skills:
  • Managing startle response, low-altitude task saturation, and forced landing field selection[cite: 1].
  • Maintaining precise slow flight control and executing clean, immediate stall recoveries[cite: 1].
FAA Links: AFH Ch 5 (Slow Flight & Stalls) AFH Ch 18 (Emergency Procedures)
