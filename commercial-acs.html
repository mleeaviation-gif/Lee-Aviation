Area of Operation I: Preflight Preparation

Task A: Pilot Qualifications
  • Knowledge: Certification requirements, recent flight experience, and logbook recordkeeping[cite: 1].
  • Knowledge: Commercial privileges, limitations, and medical certificate classes, expirations, and temporary disqualifications[cite: 1].
  • Knowledge: Documents required to exercise commercial pilot privileges and Part 68 BasicMed privileges/limitations[cite: 1].
  • Risk Management: Proficiency versus currency[cite: 1].
  • Risk Management: Flying unfamiliar aircraft or operating with unfamiliar flight display systems and avionics[cite: 1].
  • Skills: Apply requirements to act as pilot-in-command (PIC) under VFR in a scenario given by the evaluator[cite: 1].
References: 14 CFR Part 61, 14 CFR Part 91
Task B: Airworthiness Requirements
  • Knowledge: Location and expiration dates of required aircraft certificates and required inspections/logbook documentation[cite: 1].
  • Knowledge: Airworthiness Directives (ADs), Special Airworthiness Information Bulletins (SAIBs), and special flight permits[cite: 1].
  • Knowledge: Pilot-performed preventive maintenance under 14 CFR Part 43[cite: 1].
  • Knowledge: Equipment requirements for day and night VFR flight (§ 91.205), including MELs, KOELs, and required placards[cite: 1].
  • Risk Management: Mitigating risks associated with inoperative equipment discovered prior to flight[cite: 1].
  • Skills: Locate and describe airplane airworthiness and registration info, and determine airworthiness in a given scenario[cite: 1].
References: 14 CFR § 91.205, 14 CFR Part 43
Task C: Weather Information
  • Knowledge: Weather data sources (NWS, Flight Service) and preflight products (METAR/SPECI, PIREPs, Surface Analysis, TAFs, GFA, FBs, AIRMETs, SIGMETs)[cite: 1].
  • Knowledge: Meteorology applicable to VFR flight: atmospheric stability, windshear, mountain waves, fronts, icing, and thunderstorms[cite: 1].
  • Knowledge: Flight deck instrument displays of digital weather and aeronautical information[cite: 1].
  • Risk Management: Go/no-go and continue/divert decisions, personal weather minimums, and forecast turbulence/icing aloft[cite: 1].
  • Skills: Use available weather resources to obtain briefings, analyze weather conditions, and correlate info for go/no-go decisions[cite: 1].
References: PHAK Ch 12 (Weather), PHAK Ch 13 (Services)
Task D: Cross-Country Flight Planning
  • Knowledge: Route planning, airspace/SUA considerations, and navigation/communication system selection (including EFB use)[cite: 1].
  • Knowledge: Altitude selection accounting for terrain, obstacles, glide distance, and wind[cite: 1].
  • Knowledge: Calculations for time, fuel requirements/reserves, true airspeed, groundspeed, and ETA/UTC[cite: 1].
  • Knowledge: VFR flight plan elements, filing/activating/closing procedures, and inflight intercept protocols[cite: 1].
  • Risk Management: Pilot, aircraft, environment, external pressures, ATC limitations, and EFB constraints[cite: 1].
  • Skills: Prepare and present a cross-country flight plan with risk analysis, navigation log, and fuel reserve calculations[cite: 1].
References: PHAK Ch 16 (Navigation)
Task E: National Airspace System
  • Knowledge: Airspace classes, associated requirements, limitations, and chart symbols[cite: 1].
  • Knowledge: Special Use Airspace (SUA), Special Flight Rules Areas (SFRA), TFRs, and Special VFR requirements[cite: 1].
  • Risk Management: Identifying and mitigating risks associated with various airspace classes and special rules[cite: 1].
  • Skills: Comply with VFR weather minimums, communication requirements, and special air traffic rules (SATR)[cite: 1].
References: PHAK Ch 15 (Airspace)
Task F: Performance and Limitations
  • Knowledge: Use of performance charts, tables, and data affected by atmosphere, pilot technique, configuration, and airport environment[cite: 1].
  • Knowledge: Weight and balance principles, aircraft loading, and underlying flight aerodynamics[cite: 1].
  • Risk Management: Use of performance data, aircraft limitations, and differences between calculated vs. actual performance[cite: 1].
  • Skills: Compute weight and balance, correct out-of-CG errors, and use appropriate performance charts[cite: 1].
References: PHAK Ch 11 (Performance)
Task G: Operation of Systems
  • Knowledge: Flight controls, powerplant/propellers, landing gear, fuel/hydraulic/electrical systems, avionics, pitot-static, and environmental systems[cite: 1].
  • Knowledge: Indications of and procedures for managing system abnormalities or failures[cite: 1].
  • Risk Management: Detection, management, and monitoring of system malfunctions and automated systems[cite: 1].
  • Skills: Operate at least three specified aircraft systems appropriately and complete checklists[cite: 1].
References: PHAK Ch 6 (Aircraft Systems)
Task H: Human Factors
  • Knowledge: Aeromedical issues: hypoxia, hyperventilation, ear/sinus blocks, spatial disorientation, motion sickness, and carbon monoxide[cite: 1].
  • Knowledge: Fatigue, stress, dehydration, nutritional factors, and alcohol/drug regulations[cite: 1].
  • Knowledge: Aeronautical Decision-Making (ADM), Crew Resource Management (CRM), and Single-Pilot Resource Management (SRM)[cite: 1].
  • Risk Management: Physiological issues, hazardous attitudes, distractions, task prioritization, and confirmation/expectation bias[cite: 1].
  • Skills: Associate symptoms/effects for medical conditions with corrective actions, and perform self-assessment/personal minimums[cite: 1].
References: PHAK Ch 17 (Aeromedical Factors)
Task I: Water and Seaplane Characteristics (ASES/AMES)
  • Knowledge: Water surface characteristics (size, wind, current, debris, waves) and float/hull construction performance effects[cite: 1].
  • Knowledge: Causes of porpoising and skipping, and pilot actions needed to prevent/correct them[cite: 1].
  • Knowledge: Seaplane base restrictions, right-of-way/maritime rules, and marine navigation aids[cite: 1].
  • Risk Management: Local conditions, marine traffic impact, right-of-way rules, and limited base services[cite: 1].
  • Skills: Assess water characteristics, identify base restrictions/nav aids, and explain float performance effects[cite: 1].
References: Airplane Flying Handbook (Seaplane Ops)
